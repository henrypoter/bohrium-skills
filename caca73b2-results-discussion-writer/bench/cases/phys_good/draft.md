## Results
The heat-treated alloy reached a tensile strength of 612 MPa±14 MPa [P1], significantly higher than the untreated control of 540 MPa±11 MPa [P2] (t(12)=2.78, p=0.017 [P3]) (Fig. 1, Table 1).
Elongation showed an upward trend (8.9% vs 9.4% [P5][P6]) but the difference was not significant (p=0.41 [P7]) (Fig. 2).
Specimens were austenitized at 650 °C [P8] with a cooling rate of 12.5 °C/s [P9]; the measured density was 7.85 g/cm³ [P10]. Each group contained 7 specimens [P4].

## Discussion
### Main finding
The main finding is that the heat treatment significantly increased tensile strength [P1] (p=0.017 [P3]).
### Comparison with literature
The strength gain is consistent with the literature [C1], whereas the unchanged elongation differs from [C2], possibly owing to differences in cooling rates.
### Mechanism (hypothesis)
The strength increase suggests grain refinement during rapid cooling (mechanism hypothesis, to be confirmed by microscopy).
### Limitations
The limitations include the small sample size (n=7) [P4], a single cooling rate, and the lack of fractographic measurement; the statistical power is limited.
